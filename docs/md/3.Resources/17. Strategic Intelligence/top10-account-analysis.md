## 7. Market Context: What's Happening Outside the Business

These findings don't exist in a vacuum. The external environment is providing significant headwinds.

### South African Paint & Hardware Market
- **Hardware, Paint & Glass retail declined 4.3% YoY** as of November 2024 (Statistics SA) — the only major retail category to contract while others grew.
- **DIY/Hardware store segment: 0% CAGR projected 2024–2029** (Statista) — structural flatness, not cyclical dip.
- **Decorative coatings market: 1.9% CAGR 2025–2030** (Grand View Research) — modest growth at best; essentially inflation-level.

### Construction Sector (Primary Paint Demand Driver)
- SA construction **contracted 5% in real terms in 2024** and is projected to contract a further 0.5% in 2025 (ResearchAndMarkets).
- Rebound of ~2.9% average annual growth expected 2025–2028 — recovery is coming but slowly and from a low base.
- Government's USD 54.5 billion infrastructure commitment is real but mainly industrial/utilities, not residential repaint — which is Olympic's core market.

### Consumer & Macro Conditions
- Consumer confidence fell to **−20 points in Q1 2025** (down from −6 in Q4 2024).
- Real household disposable income growth: **1.6% in 2025** vs. 1.0% in 2024 — improving but still tight.
- South Africans increasingly drawing down retirement savings for daily expenses — a sign of genuine financial stress in the trade customer base.
- **Implication:** Independent hardware stores serving contractors and community builders are seeing their end-customers squeezed. When a contractor can't get a job, he doesn't buy paint.

### Competitive Landscape Risks
- **Crest Gloss Enamel at R239/5L wholesale** vs. Olympic High Gloss at R414/5L list means Crest is 42% cheaper at like-for-like comparison. On accounts that are heavily enamel-dependent (Brits, Dada's), any rep who falls behind on relationship management creates an opening.
- **Splash Paints SA** is actively building in Limpopo and exporting to Zimbabwe — the geographic zone that includes several top-10 accounts (Patel / Nkomazi is a strong signal; "Nkomazi" is in Mpumalanga bordering Mozambique, directly in Splash's expansion corridor).
- **Eclipse** is confirmed in at least one account (GG 104) where they have displaced Olympic enamel entirely. This pattern may be repeating elsewhere without field intelligence capturing it.
- **Duram's** tinting system (ColorPro, 90 colours) in national chains is a different threat — it makes the independent hardware stores look range-limited by comparison when consumers want colour matching.

## 8. The Three Hypotheses to Test

Based on the data, there are three competing explanations for the FY2026 softening. They are not mutually exclusive — all three are likely contributing, to different degrees in different accounts.

### Hypothesis A: Market is down (macro headwind)
**Evidence for:** Hardware/paint retail declined 4.3% nationally. Construction contracted. Consumer confidence at −20. All 7 declining accounts declining simultaneously.  
**Evidence against:** 3 accounts are growing strongly in the same environment.  
**Verdict:** Real, but not the whole story. The market headwind is a multiplier on existing weaknesses, not the primary cause by itself.

### Hypothesis B: Competitors eating wallet share at specific accounts
**Evidence for:** Crest is 42% cheaper on enamel. Splash Paints active in Nkomazi corridor. Eclipse displaced Olympic at one confirmed account. Dainty's Wholesale (−49% run rate, heavy below-RB pricing) buys a broad mix — a wholesaler switching supplier would create exactly this pattern.  
**Evidence against:** No direct field intelligence confirming competitor wins at specific top-10 accounts (yet).  
**Verdict:** Probable at Dainty's, possible at Builders Direct and Del Piero. Needs field verification.

### Hypothesis C: Rep/pricing/relationship breakdown causing customer drift
**Evidence for:** HGM Steelboys (91.7% below RB) is already flagged as a problem account in customer intelligence, yet is declining in volume. Builders Direct Depot's extremely low order frequency makes it fragile to any relationship break. Brits Hardware has 68.4% below-RB lines — reps are selling at a loss and the account is still declining.  
**Evidence against:** Kit Kat (best pricing discipline) is only slightly declining — their pricing relationship is clearly healthier.  
**Verdict:** Likely contributing at HGM, Builders Direct, and Dada's. Over-discounting creates a spiral: price goes down, value perception drops, account shops around anyway.

## 10. The Analysis Plan: What to Do Next

This section converts the hypotheses into actionable diagnostic steps.

### Step 1 — Field Verification Visits (Weeks 1–3, all reps)
**Objective:** Confirm or rule out competitor encroachment at the 4 critical accounts.  
**For each of Builders Direct, Dainty's, Dada's, HGM:**
- Visit in person; do a shelf audit (what brands are stocked, at what facings?)
- Ask directly: "Are you buying any paint from other suppliers currently?"
- Photograph competitor products on shelf
- Check stock levels of Olympic products — if unusually high, they may be sitting on a build and pausing orders; if low/absent, they may have already switched
- Note if any Olympic SKUs have been delisted or moved to a secondary location

### Step 2 — Brits Hardware Deep Dive (Week 1, priority)
**Objective:** Understand the March 2026 R2.56M spike and May 2026 drop-off.  
- Was March a seasonal build? A one-time bulk order for a specific project? A promotional deal?
- What is their actual monthly sell-through of Olympic products?
- If March was a pre-season stock build, the May figure (R255K) may reflect normal summer seasonality, not decline
- If March was a relationship-driven bulk deal, we need to understand what triggered it and whether it can be repeated

### Step 3 — Kit Kat Commercial Review (Week 2)
**Objective:** Understand the 135.6% FY2025 growth and whether FY2026 represents normalisation or decline.  
- How many store locations does Kit Kat operate and did any open/close in FY2025?
- Did they launch a new product range or department in FY2025 that drove the growth?
- What is their current inventory level of Olympic products?
- Is the FY2026 decline (−13.8% run rate) consistent with their overall business activity or is it Olympic-specific?

### Step 4 — Pricing Reset Conversations (Weeks 2–4)
**Objective:** Stop selling at below-rock-bottom prices, which is destroying margin without buying loyalty.  
- **HGM Steelboys:** 91.7% of lines below RB. Convene a management-level meeting. Present the data. Offer a structured contract pricing agreement with defined volume tiers — replace discretionary rep discounting with a formal pricing schedule. If they won't accept contract pricing at RB+5%, they are not a viable account at current terms.
- **Dada's World:** 83.3% below RB. Same approach. The account has been flat for two years and is now declining — the discounting is not working.
- **Builders Direct, Del Piero, Dainty's:** All in the 67–75% range. Rep coaching and management sign-off requirement on any below-RB invoice.

### Step 5 — Competitive Intelligence Gathering (Ongoing)
**Objective:** Build a picture of which competitors are winning at which accounts.  
- Deploy the verification form system (already in `_verification/`) specifically targeting the 4 critical accounts
- Brief reps on the specific competitors to watch: Crest (enamel), Splash Paints SA (QD enamel, Mpumalanga/Limpopo), Duram (PVA, national chains)
- Activate the field intake folder (`_field-intake/`) — reps should photograph any competitor products seen at Olympic accounts on every visit
- Track whether Dainty's Wholesale is stocking Crest or Stevensons products

### Step 6 — Monthly Revenue Tracking Dashboard (Week 3)
**Objective:** Real-time early warning system for the top 10.  
- Build a monthly run-rate tracker for each of the 10 accounts, comparing current 3-month rolling average to the prior year equivalent period
- Set alert thresholds: −15% = yellow flag, −30% = red flag, requiring management review
- Include pricing discipline score (% of lines below RB) alongside revenue trend
- Consider building this as a live artifact connected to the sales invoice data

### Step 7 — Macro Sensitivity Modelling (Month 2)
**Objective:** Separate market decline from account-specific problems.  
- Compare top-10 account growth rates to the South African retail trade index (StatsSA publishes monthly)
- If top-10 accounts are declining at the same rate as the market (hardware, paint & glass: −4.3% as of Nov 2024), the problem is structural. If they are declining faster, the problem is competitive or relational.
- Build a simple model: what would revenue look like if these accounts declined at the market rate vs. the actual observed rate? The gap is the addressable opportunity.

## 12. Key Unknowns That This Analysis Cannot Answer

These gaps require field intelligence or additional data to resolve:

- [ ] **What is each account's total paint spend?** (Including competitor brands.) Without this, we cannot calculate wallet share. An account spending R4M with Olympic and R8M with competitors is very different from an account where Olympic has 80% share. A rep survey or direct customer conversation is the only way to get this.
- [ ] **What drove Kit Kat's 135.6% growth in FY2025?** Store openings? A specific project? A relationship change? This is the most important unexplained number in the dataset.
- [ ] **Is Builders Direct building stock or switching suppliers?** A stock build (pause-then-resume) is very different from a supplier switch (permanent loss). Can only be answered by a site visit.
- [ ] **Are competitors actively pitching the top-10 accounts?** We know Splash Paints SA is growing in Mpumalanga/Limpopo. Crest pricing gives any enamel-heavy account a compelling reason to try them. But we have no confirmed wins at named top-10 accounts.
- [ ] **What is the actual sell-through rate at these stores?** Invoice data tells us what we sell to the store; it doesn't tell us whether the store is selling it or stockpiling it. High invoice volume + low sell-through = a pending return/credit problem.
